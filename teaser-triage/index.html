Teaser Triage

Rungta Advisors receives decks of cross-border deal teasers, one opportunity per slide. Teaser Triage reads every slide and shortlists the ones where an Indian buyer, seller or investor could fit, and it is built so it never quietly drops a deal an analyst would want.

The problem

A teaser deck can run to 60 slides, and most opportunities have nothing to do with India. Reading every slide carefully is slow; skimming risks missing the one opportunity that matters, and in M&A advisory a missed deal costs far more than an extra minute of reading.

So the brief was not "find the relevant slides". It was "cut the reading without ever losing a relevant slide".

How it works

  1. Split the deck

    The PDF is split into slides. Each slide's text is extracted and an image is rendered, and slides that are mostly pictures are flagged.

  2. Ask narrow questions

    A small decision model (TypeSafe's Jev) answers 13 specific questions per slide: deal type, India angle, buyer regions, company region, business type, profitability, sector, revenue band and market.

  3. Route with rules

    Plain code turns those answers into a route. A hard rule, such as 'buyer regions listed without India', can skip a slide only when the model is at least 90% sure and the slide shows no Indian angle.

  4. Escalate the unclear ones

    Slides the rules can't settle go to Claude Haiku with the slide image, its text and the first model's answers. A skip must name a rule that the first model also backs; otherwise the slide stays with an analyst.

  5. Review and export

    Analysts work through three piles with keyboard shortcuts, label slides, and export the relevant ones as a branded PDF or CSV. Their labels feed the evaluation page.

Decisions I made

Recall first, everywhere

Every rule is biased toward keeping a slide. Doubtful slides go to Quick check rather than Skipped, and a skip needs two independent signals to agree.

Switched off the stronger model

A second pass with a larger model looked like an upgrade, but in testing it skipped two deals the analysts wanted. It's off by default.

Measure against analysts, not intuition

An evaluation page reports recall, precision and threshold sweeps against analyst labels. Two decks were labelled blind so improvements couldn't be tuned to the test set. Every prompt or rule change is re-checked to keep recall at 100%.

Treat teasers as confidential

Live model calls refuse to run until zero-data-retention is confirmed with both providers. Logins use argon2 hashing, rate limiting and CSRF protection, and the app stays out of search engines.

Results so far

100%Recall: all 37 slides analysts marked relevant were kept, across 132 labelled slides in four decks.
27%Of slides cleared without reading (36 of 132), none of them relevant.
$0.07Per 100 slides in model costs: about $0.016 for the first model and $0.055 for Claude. Re-runs reuse cached answers and cost nothing.
17 sTo triage a 31-slide deck end to end.

What I'd do next

  • Shrink Quick check. 45 of its 50 slides weren't relevant. Tighter per-sector rules could send more of them to Skipped, as long as recall stays at 100%.
  • Label more decks per sector. A sector needs at least two labelled decks before its numbers mean much, and so far each has one.
  • Hand the shortlist to a matching step that suggests specific Indian buyers or targets for each relevant opportunity.
